The prompt (verbatim)

creditPrompt by @FrancoE114696: source post
guideRun it yourself, step by step · the Short · all episodes · data
I want you to draw ONE masterpiece: Starship on Mars. A single image, 16:9, drawn only with JavaScript. Highly detailed. Put your soul and all your energy into it.

WHAT YOU DRAW

Starship arriving at Mars or lifting off from Mars. You choose which one, whichever gives the stronger image. Only the ship goes to Mars, without the booster and without a launch tower. It has to be recognizable as the real Starship, not just any rocket: the stainless steel, the flaps, the black hexagonal heat shield tiles, the engines lit.

The framing, the time of day and the point of view are up to you. I'd rather be surprised than get the obvious postcard.

I want to feel the scale and the moment: something enormous made by people, touching an entire planet that is empty. No people, no astronauts, no faces. No text or logos.

THE COLOR

Colorful and luminous. I don't want a dull brown Mars or a black sky that swallows the image. Saturated colors and light you can feel: the fire of the engines, the glowing dust, the sky. Mars gives you real material for this (for example, its sunsets are blue). Use it or make something up.

The look: the colors of 90s anime, those hand-painted backgrounds from science fiction OVAs. But it shouldn't look like a frame from any anime. Something original, very creative, that blows my mind. Use every pixel of the canvas, leave no dead areas.

HOW IT HAS TO FEEL

It has to look drawn BY HAND by a person, on real paper. Nothing digital, nothing that looks like an SVG or a render. Pick between 3 and 5 dry tools (colored pencils, pastel, charcoal, chalk, crayon, whatever combination works best) or invent your own tools and colors. The paper is your choice too: color, grain, light or dark. Find the combination that makes the color shine.

The human quality comes from building up thousands of strokes, not from filling in shapes. Each stroke with its own pressure, tremor and direction. Pigment that catches on the grain of the paper and leaves gaps. Smudges from resting your hand. Construction lines left behind. Edges that don't close. No smooth gradients, perfect circles, ruler-straight lines or filter glows. On paper no color ever sits evenly.

THE EMOTIONS

The drawing has to convey what these two stories feel like. But do NOT draw anything that appears in them: no objects, no places, no people. Only the emotion.

===

You spent two years building something nobody quite understood. It's the early hours of the morning, you're alone, you press the button and you stare at the screen without breathing. Ten seconds that last an hour. And it works. First try. You stand up so fast you knock over the chair. You can't shout because everyone is asleep, so you step out onto the balcony in your socks and laugh to yourself like an idiot. Your phone starts buzzing and doesn't stop. One of the messages is from someone you've admired for years, and all it says is "saw it. incredible". You go down to the street because you can't stay still. The bakery on the corner is just opening and the baker gives you the first croissants, still warm, because he says you look like something good just happened to you. You walk back home and hit every green light.

===

You're 5 or 6, you don't remember exactly. Your family drove all night and you slept in the back seat, covered with a jacket. Your dad wakes you up and says "look". You get out barefoot, the sand is cold, and there's the sea for the first time. It's so big it doesn't fit in your eyes. It scares you and makes you laugh at the same time. You run to the water and run back when the wave comes, a thousand times. Your mom calls you because they bought churros. You eat them with your hands covered in sand and sugar and you couldn't care less, and before you've finished the last one you're already running back to the water because you can't wait.

===

BEFORE YOU DRAW

Do your research, so it's believable:
- Search the web for real, recent photos of Starship. Check which version flew most recently: it changed quite a bit from one version to the next, and what you remember may be out of date. Look at proportions, flaps, tiles, engines and how the steel reflects light.
- Find the official renders of Starship on Mars, and real photos of Mars taken by the rovers: the ground, the rocks, the dust, the color of the sky at different times of day.
- Download the best ones to a references folder and open them to actually look at them. Without opening a browser.
- They're a guide. Don't trace any photo or copy a composition: the image is yours.

HOW TO WORK

- Run everything with Node and write a 3840×2160 PNG. Don't use a browser or headless Chrome: it eats up my machine's memory.
- You can use subagents, but all of them on the same model as you.
- After each render, open the PNG and look at it with your own eyes, whole and in 100% crops. Critique it like a demanding art director: does it look handmade? is it colorful and luminous? are there empty or weak areas? can you feel the emotion? Fix it and render again. Don't stop at the first version that's "fine". Stop when it moves you.
- When you're done, don't leave any process running.
- Save the final as starship-marte.png in the working folder, show it to me, and tell me in a few lines which moment you chose and what tools you drew it with.

Good luck.
