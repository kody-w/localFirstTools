guideRun it yourself, step by step · the Short · all episodes · data
Create a cinematic, photorealistic view of an ancient interdimensional portal activating within monumental obsidian and weathered basalt ruins in a self-contained HTML file. Generate the scene entirely through code, including all geometry, materials, textures, lighting, and effects, without importing images or 3D models or requiring external runtime dependencies. Create an original archaeological structure grounded in real geological formations, stone construction techniques, erosion, and physically plausible environmental materials. The camera glides slowly around the portal while a coherent alternate environment becomes visible through its aperture, surrounding dust responds to its energy, and light and space distort around its boundary. Make the camera interactive: click and drag to orbit through 360 degrees, scroll to zoom in and out, and smoothly resume the automatic glide after a few seconds of no input. Support touch too (drag to orbit, pinch to zoom). Choose the techniques yourself, then render, visually inspect, and iteratively improve the architectural geometry, volcanic glass, basalt fractures, weathering, moss, contact shadows, spatial distortion, viewpoint-correct portal depth and parallax, environmental light interaction, reflections, atmospheric scattering, restrained energy effects, geological scale, and camera movement until they achieve convincing feature-film photorealism. Make the fictional portal feel physically present within an otherwise realistic environment rather than resembling a glowing flat surface. Verify multiple viewing angles, close-ups, and responsive desktop and mobile interaction before final delivery.
